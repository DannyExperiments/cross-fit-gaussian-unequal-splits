# Q563 source and priority assessment

**Verdict, 27 November 2026.** The published source asks whether a balanced partition always minimizes the expected squared Euclidean diameter of the two-fold Gaussian cross-fit confidence set. The counterexample concerns that exact set. The checked primary sources did not disclose an earlier counterexample for this objective or an expansion for a fixed unequal split as the nominal noncoverage level tends to zero. Confidence in the bounded search is moderate.

## Exact question and conventions

Robin Dunn, Aaditya Ramdas, Sivaraman Balakrishnan and Larry Wasserman, *Gaussian universal likelihood ratio testing*, **Biometrika** 110(2), 319–337 (2023), DOI 10.1093/biomet/asac064. Section 3.1 makes the conjecture after Theorem 3. The setup is a finite IID sample from a Gaussian with mean in the full Euclidean parameter space and known identity covariance. Two fixed nonempty folds use the opposite fold's sample mean in the numerator. Their raw likelihood ratios receive equal weight even when the folds differ in size. The confidence set uses the strict threshold 1/α on that average. Section S4 of arXiv v3 (20 November 2022) states the general-split likelihood explicitly in Eq. (S28). The earlier preprint calls the simulation supplement S3. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asac064) · [Author text §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.14676v3#S3.SS1) · [Author supplement §S4](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.14676v3#S4) · [Pinned catalog Q563](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/computer_science_ai/classical-statistical-machine-learning.md#q563).

Robin Dunn, *Advances in Nonasymptotic and Nonparametric Inference*, Carnegie Mellon University doctoral dissertation, July 2021, §2.3.1 (printed p. 12) and Appendix B.3 (printed p. 125, PDF page 145), also record the wording and display the same likelihood at arbitrary split proportion. Figure B.2 and the paper's Figure S2 illustrate sets from a single simulated sample of 1,000 bivariate observations. The paper's Figure S3 uses 100 such datasets at α=0.1 and compares mean area. Neither experiment tests the expectation of squared diameter across arbitrary confidence levels. The journal metadata and the authors' full text were available. The publisher full-text route was inaccessible during this check. [Dunn dissertation Appendix B.3](https://robinmdunn.github.io/files/RobinDunn-dissertation.pdf#page=145) · [Paper supplement](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.14676v3#S4).

## Closest earlier results

- Dunn et al. Theorem 3 and §3.3 optimize the expected squared **radius of the single-split** ball. Its center and log-threshold coefficient are computed from its one testing fold. Theorem 4 bounds a **balanced cross-fit** set by a recentered split ball of squared radius \(4\log(1/\alpha)/n+D^2\), where \(D\) is the distance between the sample means. This gives a related upper bound at the balanced split. The present exact balanced squared diameter for dimension at least two follows by factoring the two exponentials and attaining the bound along a perpendicular direction. [Author text, §§3.1–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.14676v3#S3).
- Wasserman, Ramdas and Balakrishnan introduced the arithmetic-mean cross-fit likelihood ratio, Eq. (5), in *Universal inference*, PNAS 117(29), 16880–16890 (2020), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1922664117. Their diameter theorem concerns Hellinger distance for the split set. Strieder and Drton, *Electronic Journal of Statistics* 16, 6631–6650 (2022), DOI 10.1214/22-EJS2099, study local asymptotic power and split ratios. Their §5 cross-fit statistic averages twice-log split ratios. Tse and Davison, *Stat* 11, e501 (2022), DOI 10.1002/sta4.501, §5, study variance and power for the arithmetic-mean cross-fit and K-fold likelihood ratios, including uneven Gaussian folds. These analyses do not give the squared-diameter comparison or the fixed-sample high-confidence expansion. [Universal inference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7382245/) · [Strieder and Drton §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.06748#S5) · [Tse and Davison §5](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sta4.501).
- Lorenz Matz's 2025 University of Vienna master's thesis *A discussion of Universal Inference* treats unequal **single-split** sets in its Theorem 30 and surrounding volume comparisons, and reviews cross-fit simulations. *Robust universal inference for misspecified models* studies other confidence sets, including cross-fit validity and single-split versus cross-fit comparisons in §§S7–S8. Neither supplied a result for choosing fold sizes for the squared diameter of this correctly specified Gaussian arithmetic-mean set. [Matz thesis](https://services.phaidra.univie.ac.at/api/object/o%3A2144433/download) · [Biometrika asaf070](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asaf070).

## Contribution and scope

For \(n=4,d=1,\alpha=e^{-36}\), the two directional bounding balls give an expectation bound strictly below 104 for a 1–3 split and a lower bound of 120 for a 2–2 split. The Gaussian fold-mean moments give deterministic bounds on the sampling expectation. This example does not require a perpendicular direction, which is available only for the companion balanced identity when \(d\ge2\). For fixed \(n,d\), the leading slope in \(\log(1/\alpha)\) is \(8/\max(n_0,n_1)\). At a fixed unequal split the dominant ball gives an exponentially accurate two-term expression. In particular the extreme nonempty folds eventually minimize the expected squared diameter at fixed sample size. Ordinary significance levels, changing dimensions, many-fold averaging and volume optimization are separate questions.
